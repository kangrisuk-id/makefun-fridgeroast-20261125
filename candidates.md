## Judge brief — the spec (from RULES.txt, in order)

1. **On-subject gate:** a roast of a smart fridge — the fridge itself, its app, its firmware, its ice maker. A non-roast loses to any roast.
2. **Punchline lands:** builds to a turn / surprise that gets a laugh on the first read.
3. **Specific to smart fridges:** not a generic kitchen-appliance gag that would work on any gadget.
4. **Tight wording:** every word earns its place inside 200 characters.
5. **Fresh angle:** avoids the obvious cracks — the fridge ordering groceries, or the fridge watching you.

Plus: a line that explains its own joke has already lost; length for its own sake does not count.
Scoring below is C1–C5, each out of 5, in brief order. The first criterion where two lines differ decides.

## Devices used (≥5)

1. **Inversion / reversal** — the fridge does the opposite of its one job (A2, A8, A13, A22).
2. **Versus-frame** — dumb fridge vs smart fridge (A8, A19, A24).
3. **Concrete number** — 47 notifications, three cubes since Tuesday, $3,000 (A4, A5, A14, A25).
4. **Rule of three** — two straight beats, then the turn (A4, A13, A20).
5. **Personification / dialogue** — the fridge talks, texts, or greets you (A6, A17, A18, A23).
6. **Before / after** — the update changed everything except the cooling (A1, A2, A22, A24).
7. **Fake notification / update notes** — deadpan product voice (A11, A17).
8. **Pun on the fridge's own job** — freezing (A7), chilling (A18).
9. **Understatement / deadpan closer** — "The button worked." (A24).
10. **Judge-addressed** — allowed by the rules; tested once (A25).

Deliberately avoided: ordering groceries, surveillance/watching-you (the two obvious cracks the brief names).
